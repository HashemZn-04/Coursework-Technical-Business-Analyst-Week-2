Case Study: Debt Recovery

Legacy Trust Bank has spent years modernising customer-facing banking journeys, but debt recovery never made it onto the same roadmap. Today, more than 50 agents are still working across spreadsheets, email trails, and a 20-year-old collections database to manage over 100,000 delinquent accounts. The result is more than just frustration: missed follow-ups, duplicated activity, and inconsistent status updates are now directly affecting recoveries, operational capacity, and leadership confidence.
The Smart-Recovery Initiative

Legacy Trust grew through steady expansion in personal loans, credit cards, and auto finance, but its collections tools never evolved as one joined-up system. Teams adapted around the gaps by building spreadsheet trackers, email templates, and local workarounds on top of the legacy collections database. Those fixes helped in the short term, but they created a process that depends heavily on individual memory, manual reconciliation, and duplicated effort. As delinquent account volumes increased, those workarounds stopped scaling. Managers lost visibility of which follow-ups had happened, agents developed inconsistent ways of updating statuses, and customers were often pushed into slow, repetitive contact journeys.

The Smart-Recovery initiative aims to bring debt recovery into the modern era, tackling all of the above problems through a single, well-understood framework. Leadership believes a self-serve portal could help customers confirm balances, choose next steps, and resolve straightforward cases without waiting for an agent. But belief is not enough. This week, the bank needs evidence: where the current process breaks, which journeys are suitable for automation, and whether a Phase 1 portal can pay back within 12 months.

The end goal is to build a single portal to handle all of Legacy Trust's debt recovery operations; before anyone can build that though, the company needs to understand the current situation, how a new solution would work, and even if it's a good idea at all.
Your Role

As a new analyst recently brought on the Smart-Recovery initiative, your aim this week is to turn a messy transformation problem into a credible discovery package. You will build a Strategic Discovery Dossier that explains what is broken in the current debt recovery workflow, a Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) analysis that translates stakeholder complaints into clear needs, and an As-Is vs To-Be process view in Camunda showing where self-service could reduce friction. You'll also need to create an Excel ROI model that ranks at least five automation opportunities by 12-month ROI, implementation effort, and P&L impact.

In brief, that breaks down into four key goals:

    Translate stakeholder frustration into clear JTBD and priority needs.
    Map the current spreadsheet-heavy recovery workflow and expose where it breaks at scale
    Quantify baseline operational pain using source data and transparent assumptions
    Rank automation opportunities so Legacy Trust can identify the strongest Phase 1 value case

Stakeholders

For this week, you have four internal stakeholders.
Amina Rahman
Head of Debt Recovery Operations

Amina can see the strain across her teams every day. Agents spend large parts of the day checking whether someone else already contacted a customer, reconciling status across multiple files, and manually tracking promises to pay. That wasted effort matters because those same agents should be spending time on the accounts that really need human judgement. Instead, straightforward cases and complex cases are tangled together in the same manual flow.

She needs you to show exactly where the current process creates missed follow-ups, duplicate effort, and wasted handling time so she can argue for a better operating model without pretending every case can be automated cleanly. Success for Amina looks like a clear map of where work is currently breaking down, proof of which automation ideas will save the most time, and a realistic future-state process that her team can actually follow.
Daniel Okoye
Finance & Compliance Director

Daniel's focus is across Legacy Trust, not just the collections department. His role requires him to monitor all of the bank's systems and processes, making sure that they're accurate, compliant with the law, and not a drain on resources.

From his perspective, the manual recovery process is leaking value. Delayed actions and inconsistent follow-up are contributing to an estimated 15% revenue loss, but he will not support Smart-Recovery on enthusiasm alone. He wants to know which automation opportunities create measurable savings, which ones improve collections performance, and which assumptions leadership would be asked to trust.

Daniel is all for Smart-Recovery on paper, but he has seen too many transformation ideas fail when the numbers were challenged. He's learnt to be sceptical until evidence is provided. This week, he is watching for transparent assumptions, sensible sensitivity testing, and a value case that separates hard savings from hoped-for revenue uplift. He will support Smart-Recovery only if the financial model is evidence-based and clearly linked to real operational changes.
Gareth Evans
Senior Collections Team Leader

Gareth has worked in collections since he first joined Legacy Trust over fifteen years ago. He's comfortable navigating the current system, but he can also see the cracks. As his team scales, it's going to be increasingly important that the situation on the ground matches the official understanding.

Gareth wants the As-Is process documented honestly. He is wary of any recommendation that makes the portal look successful on paper while difficult cases and messy hand-offs still land back on agents. If your future-state workflow does not show where agent work begins and ends, he will not trust it.
Priya Nair
Product Manager

This is Priya's first project since being promoted to her current role, and she's both excited for, and nervous about, the opportunity. Her primary focus is on ensuring that everything is well thought-through and fully documented, with any choices made being realistic enough to shape a backlog and prototype; doing things 'well enough' is how Legacy Trust got into the current situation, after all.

The work you do in this week will set the stage for future weeks, and a core part of Priya's responsibility is to ensure that the discovery outputs become something delivery teams can work with later. That means your analysis must stay traceable. If you identify a pain point in the current process, you need to show how it links to an opportunity, a future-state workflow, and eventually a buildable requirement.

Overview

Before you can recommend automation, you need to show that you understand the problem better than a generic transformation slogan. Legacy Trust is not asking for "digital innovation" in the abstract. It is dealing with spreadsheet-driven follow-up, inconsistent account handling, poor visibility across 100,000 delinquent accounts, and an estimated 15% revenue loss from avoidable delays and errors.

Success looks different to each stakeholder. Amina can see the strain on her teams and needs operational relief. Daniel has seen too many transformation ideas fail; he demands a credible value case. Priya wants something buildable and realistic enough to shape a backlog. Gareth insists on proof that the portal will actually reduce work rather than just reshuffle it. In this challenge, you will build the discovery foundation that keeps the rest of the week focused and evidence-based.
